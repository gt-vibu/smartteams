Attendance

5 commits

A missed check-out can be repaired

d0895e1f34e451

Before: a day that ended on a check-in counted zero worked time forever. The correction workflow only moved punches that already existed, so asking for a check-out was refused, and an approved reason-only correction changed nothing.

  • The employee asks for the check-out they forgot, with the real time and a reason. It goes through the same approval policy as any other correction.
  • Only the final approval writes the OUT punch, after locking the record and re-running every check, so nothing can add a second check-out. Worked minutes come from the existing totals, and payroll over that date is marked stale through the existing helper.
  • Refused for: a colleague's day, another tenant's record, a check-out before the check-in, a future time, more than 24 hours after the check-in, a time after the employee's next punch, or while one is already awaiting a decision.
  • Nothing closes a day automatically, and no check-out time is ever invented.
  • The week arrows on the attendance screens work, and a day with no check-out reads "No check-out" instead of 00:00.

Checking in on an approved optional holiday

22b02d4

Before: the day was silently both a granted holiday and a worked day, and nothing recorded which it was.

  • The check-in is always allowed — never rejected, never silently cancelled. The day is marked as needing resolution, and the employee gives a reason plus an optional comment.
  • A manager or admin decides: keep the holiday, so the check-in stays as history but counts no worked time; or convert to a working day, which cancels the holiday selection through the existing cancellation, with its existing audit entry and event, and lets attendance stand.
  • Either way, payroll over that date is marked stale. No automatic payroll decision is made.
  • Self-resolution without permission, forged and cross-tenant ids, duplicate resolutions and two managers deciding at once are all refused. The state is explicit, not a boolean flag.
  • New table attendance_holiday_reviews, with row-level security, one pending review per day, and a constraint keeping a decided review consistent.

A day nobody checked out of is closed for review

dfebb80

Before: a forgotten check-out left the day at nothing, and nothing ever prompted anybody about it — somebody had to notice.

  • An organization switches it on and picks the closing time in its own timezone, 23:59 by default. A sweep then raises, for each open day whose own cutoff has passed, the ordinary missing-check-out correction at that time.
  • Closed means proposed, not decided. No punch is written and no record changes: the day still counts no worked minutes and no overtime until a person settles it. Nothing reaches a timesheet or a payslip because a clock struck midnight.
  • Who reviews it is the organization's existing attendance approval policy — their manager, a role, or named people, multi-step if wanted — so changing who receives these is done on the Approvals screen, not in code.
  • The approver accepts the proposed time, enters the real one, or rejects the day. A replacement time is re-checked against the day's punches and recorded on the correction; only then is the check-out written and payroll marked stale.
  • A shift that crosses midnight is never closed this way: at 23:59 it is still being worked.
  • It looks back three days so an outage catches up, never raises a second request for a day, and one organization's missing policy cannot stop the others. An administrator can also run the close on demand.

Shifts

2 commits

An assignment can be read, listed and ended

5b6c5712416af6

Before: shifts could be created and assigned, but no assignment could be read back. Home always said "Not recorded", no screen listed who was on a shift, and moving someone between shifts was impossible, because the old open-ended assignment could not be closed and the new one was refused as an overlap.

  • Home's Work Schedule shows the assigned shift and its hours, or states plainly that none is assigned — in neutral colours rather than the old red error styling. The Dashboard tile lost its invented "On Schedule" badge.
  • Each shift now has a People dialog: who is on it, labelled Current, Upcoming or Ended against the tenant's own date, with a "Show ended" toggle and paging.
  • End assignment asks for the employee's last day on the shift and a reason. The row is locked, a day before the start is refused, and the end only ever moves earlier — never later, which could overlap a later assignment.
  • An overlap refusal in the Assign dialog now says how to fix it.
  • Every end is audited as SHIFT_ASSIGNMENT_ENDED with its reason. Like retiring a shift, which already ends its open assignments, it publishes no federation event.

Timesheets and Log Time

4 commits

Hours are asked for, not assumed

0ab4bae19c1ab366fd8a83b23af4

Before: Log Time opened with 08:00 already entered, so saving without looking recorded a full day whatever had been worked — and logged time feeds approval and pay.

  • Hours start empty in both modes, and saving asks for them. A job type that did not exist is no longer filled in for you.
  • Quick-adding a project from Log Time is visible and works. It now requires projects.write, closing a second, weaker path to creating organization-wide projects.
  • A single day's logged time cannot exceed 24 hours; one 100,000-minute entry had been accepted.
  • An approver can no longer approve their own timesheet. Opening or re-deriving a period needs timesheets.read.all, since it resets every sheet in the period and withdraws approvals.
  • Submission requires a configured approval policy with resolvable approvers, so a decision can never skip approver checks.
  • Managers hold timesheets.decide everywhere now: the seed granted a permission the catalogue does not define, so managers saw the approval screen and were refused by the API.

Giving an employee a login

1 commit

An access code, not a password the administrator knows

968458f

Before: the panel that issues access codes was written, but nothing rendered it — so the only way to give someone a login was onboarding's temporary password, which the administrator reads.

  • The employee record carries it now: whether they can sign in yet, and the one action that moves them forward. The code is shown once, expires in 48 hours and is stored only as a digest; issuing a new one withdraws the old.
  • The employee enters it and chooses their own email and password, so no permanent credential is ever known to two people.

Security and data isolation

3 commits

Ownership on every self-service write

3b23af466fd8a8d371061

Before: self-service writes trusted the employee, timesheet and attendance ids in the request. Every seeded employee holds the write permissions, and the services only checked that the target was in the same organization — so a colleague's UUID was enough to act as them.

  • A shared write boundary resolves the employee from the session, and allows an explicit id only for callers holding that module's existing .read.all breadth. No new permission, no backfill.
  • Native punches are recorded at server time, with the work date derived in the organization's timezone, instead of taking both from the request.
  • File deletion, uploads and upload completion use the same self-or-breadth model as list and download; payslips and payroll exports need breadth.
  • Row-level security is enforced in local development: a least-privilege runtime role is provisioned, and tools that genuinely need elevated access ask for it by their own variable instead of borrowing DATABASE_URL.
  • Hard-coded database credential fallbacks were removed from three scripts.
  • A path id that is not a UUID answered 500 — Postgres refused it and nothing mapped the error. It answers 404 now.

No Docker, no MinIO

The local API stays up, and typecheck stops killing it

4e697af4f4250a402fcd6
  • Typecheck and build no longer regenerate the Prisma client unconditionally. That rewrote 97 committed files, and the dev watcher killed the API mid-request — six times in one day.
  • The dev server owns its process: it restarts after a clean build and after an unasked exit, printing how it died, and gives up after five exits in a minute.

The six new end-to-end suites are authorization-boundaries, ownership-and-approvals, missing-checkout, holiday-checkin, shift-assignment and auto-checkout. Each drives real sessions through the real API, including cross-tenant and self-scope refusals.
